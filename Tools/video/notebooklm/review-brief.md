# Prdivý závod (Fart Race) – briefing for a review show

*Source material for a podcast-style review. Everything under "Facts" is true.
Everything under "Running jokes" is invented on purpose and may be quoted as
such.*

## Facts

**What it is.** Prdivý závod – Czech for "Fart Race" – is a small, free,
open-source first-person game made with Unity 6. It runs on Linux, Windows,
macOS and Android phones. The game's own texts are in Czech.

**What you do.** You run around a four-storey concrete car park. Glowing green
clouds float all over it: those are farts. You walk into them to collect them
in a backpack that holds eight. Then you carry them to the goal – an orange
extraction hood in a corner of the ground floor – and expel them there. The
first competitor to deliver 15 farts wins. At the end a banner shows the final
ranking.

**Who you play against.** Two bots: Pšouk, in red, and Bzdina, in blue. (Both
names are Czech words for a small fart.) They collect, use the ramps between
the decks, deliver, and blast you when you carry a lot.

**The blast.** For two farts from your backpack you can blast a rival who is
in your sights. They vanish in a green cloud for ten seconds. Bots blast too,
a little less hard: five seconds.

**The monster.** About 45 seconds into every match the ceiling lamps start to
flicker, the sunny day turns into a green gloom, and the fart monster boils up
out of the floor – preferably behind you. It is a tall, horned phantom made of
gas, with burning eyes and far too many teeth. It drifts straight through
columns and railings, and it gets faster the longer it hunts: walking will not
save you, sprinting will. If it catches you, it roars in your face, eats your
entire backpack and knocks you out for five seconds. You can drive it off for a
while by blasting it – which also costs two farts. It follows you to other
floors by sinking through the ground.

**Two players.** Two people can play the same match on two devices: one sets
up a game and shows a short code, the other joins on the same Wi-Fi. Two
Android phones can also do it over Bluetooth.

**Controls.** On a computer: W A S D, mouse, Shift to sprint, Space to jump.
On a phone: a virtual stick under the left thumb, dragging on the right to look
around, and two buttons – SKOK (jump) and PRD! (fart!).

**The sound.** The game uses 63 real fart recordings, cut from one tape in the
sound archive of the Finnish broadcaster Yle, recorded in 1986 and published
under a Creative Commons licence. The monster's voice is computed by the game:
its growl is one of those recordings played three times too slowly.

**The look.** Photographic lighting: a real sky photo, sun and shadows that
match it, wet-looking concrete, reflections. The whole car park is built by
program code the moment the game starts; the level file is empty.

**How it was made.** The models – the round little racers with gas tanks on
their backs, the monster, the railings – are generated by scripts. There is a
lighter version for old phones. The project has documentation in English and
Czech and an MIT licence. It can be found at github.com/xjrk58/unity-hra.

## Running jokes the hosts may use

- Made-up press quotes: "A triumph of the genre. There is no genre." (The
  Daily Whiff). "I screamed. My phone screamed. The neighbours have moved
  out." (Flatulence Monthly). "The concrete is so realistic I checked my
  shoes." (Car Park Enthusiast Weekly).
- The backpack holds eight farts and science cannot explain how.
- The extraction hood "has seen things".
- Pšouk blasted the reviewer from behind and has not been forgiven.
- The monster "hates the taste of its own medicine".
- Verdict: twelve out of ten. "A breath of fresh air. Technically the opposite."

## Tone

Family-friendly. Silly, warm, wildly over-enthusiastic – as if this were the
most important game release of the decade. No crude language beyond the word
"fart".
